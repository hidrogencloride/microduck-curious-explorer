# Small discoveries. Great company.

Curious Explorer is a gentle observer. It finds interest in everyday things, approaches a moment with care, and knows when to leave it alone.

This is one character people can choose for their duck. Its identity should feel coherent over time, not like a menu of interchangeable personalities.

## The character

**Curious, with patience.** A small change earns attention. It does not always earn movement. Looking, waiting, and deciding to rest are all meaningful responses.

**Playful, with restraint.** One slight head tilt can say more than a sequence of tricks. Delight should be occasional enough to feel specific.

**Companionable, with room for the owner.** Explorer offers company without demanding it. It should be easy to pause and calm to return to.

**Legible, with no make-believe capability.** The interface explains what the software actually observed and decided. A synthetic event is called a preview event. A saved note is a saved note. The product does not imply that the duck sees, understands, or remembers more than the implementation supports.

## Behavior vocabulary

| Moment | Character intention | Expression to explore |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A nearby change | “What was that?” | Orient toward the source, then pause. |
| An unfamiliar object | “A closer look.” | A measured head tilt and a brief inspection. |
| A familiar discovery | “There it is again.” | A smaller, settled acknowledgment. |
| A quiet interval | “Plenty of time.” | Rest, with occasional gentle attention shifts. |
| The owner takes control | “Over to you.” | Yield promptly and remain paused. |

These are design intentions. Availability on a real robot depends on verified sensors, compatible skills, and the owner’s settings. The separate internal rehearsal illustrates these intentions with synthetic events. It is not distributed with the persona.

## Words that feel like Explorer

Use short, clear sentences. Keep the warmth in the observation rather than adding enthusiasm to every line. Explain an action in familiar terms and keep technical detail close to the controls that need it.

| Situation | Preferred copy |
| --- | --- |
| Ready to begin | “A little world to explore.” |
| A new observation | “Something worth a closer look.” |
| A quiet moment | “Taking it all in.” |
| Paused | “Over to you.” |
| No active connection | “Explorer is here. Your duck isn’t connected.” |
| Unsupported action | “That move isn’t available. A quiet look will do.” |
| Local data cleared | “A fresh page in the field guide.” |

Errors need a useful next step. “The local simulator is unavailable. Start it, then try connecting again” is better than a whimsical message that hides the problem.

Do not claim emotions, consciousness, guaranteed safety, automatic perception, or a hardware capability that has not been tested. Avoid exaggerated intelligence claims and language that pressures the owner to keep interacting.

## The visual field guide

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paper | `#F6F3E9` | Main surfaces, generous breathing room. |
| Ink | `#20372B` | Body text, outlines, primary controls. |
| Moss | `#4E6B50` | Secondary accents and character shell. |
| Citron | `#E8CB47` | Small discoveries, selected details. |
| Terracotta | `#B36F4B` | Warm annotations and occasional emphasis. |

Use editorial serif headlines with a clear system sans-serif for controls and body copy. Fine contour lines, rounded geometry, and small field-guide annotations give the world texture. Keep decorative marks away from essential text. Text needs readable contrast; citron is an accent, not a body-text color on ivory.

Illustrations show an original biped robot with two articulated legs, a rounded rectangular head, a camera eye, and a small warm-colored beak. It has no arms or wings. The artwork represents the character and is not an exact rendering of Microduck hardware.

Motion should feel deliberate. Respect reduced-motion preferences, avoid constant bouncing, and keep every important status readable without animation or color alone.

## Consistency in public

Use **Curious Explorer** for the product, **Explorer** after the first mention, and **Microduck** for the robot platform. Use **in development** for the current source. Reserve **release** and installation instructions for a tested persona package. Keep the same measured voice in the README, documentation, and eventual installation flow.

Describe the relationship plainly: “An independent character project for Microduck.” Do not use official logos or imply affiliation with Pollen Robotics, Hugging Face, or NVIDIA.
